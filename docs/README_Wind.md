# Local wind sea in BinWaves

BinWaves propagates the offshore spectrum to the coast by linear superposition of boundary cases (`build_kp` / `reconstruct`).
Waves generated by the wind *inside* the model domain (a bay, a lagoon, a harbour, a semi-enclosed sea) are missing from
that: they never crossed the boundary. Wind growth is not linear in the wind speed, and its fetch geometry depends on the
wind direction, so it cannot be another set of superposed cases. It is handled by a look-up table instead.

## Method
1. **Training** (`build_wind_kp`): one stationary solution per (wind direction, wind speed), uniform wind over the domain,
   no incoming waves (`ZeroBC()` on every side), wind input and whitecapping on (whitecapping is what bounds a stationary
   wind sea), the model's other physics as set (breaking, friction, refraction, currents, GSE). Each run stores the
   wind-sea m0 field (m²) and the full spectrum at the output points.
2. **Prediction** (`wind_sea`, or `reconstruct(...; wind, U10, wdir)`): for each time
   * direction: linear in angle between the two neighbouring trained directions (circular);
   * speed: between the two neighbouring trained speeds, either a power law (`:loglog`, default: log m0 linear in log U)
     or a straight line (`:linear`). Outside the trained range the end segment is extrapolated (log-log slope limited to
     m0 ∝ U⁴; `:linear` goes to zero at U = 0 below the lowest speed). Below `umin` (1 m/s) there is no wind sea.
   * point spectra: the bracketing spectra are blended and rescaled to the interpolated variance.
3. **Combination**: the wind sea is added as variance, `Hs² = Hs²_swell + Hs²_wind`, and the point spectra are summed
   (`point_parameters` works on the total or on `rec.wind.point_var` alone). The direction vectors (Σ E cos θ, Σ E sin θ)
   are added too, so `rec.Dir` and `rec.Spread` describe the combined sea state. For the wind sea alone the vector is
   interpolated in speed as a normalised vector and scaled by the interpolated m0. In the test, the combined mean
   direction is within 0.67° RMS of a coupled direct run (2.3° if the wind sea is ignored).

```julia
wk  = build_wind_kp(model; dirs = 0:22.5:337.5, speeds = [5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30], points = pts,
                    checkpoint = "wind.nc")                     # resumable, like build_kp
wk  = load_wind_kp_netcdf("wind.nc")
rec = reconstruct(kp, E, f, dir; wind = wk, U10 = u10, wdir = wdir)   # u10, wdir: one value per time of E
rec.Hs                 # swell + wind sea
rec.wind.Hs            # wind sea alone (nx, ny, nt)
```
Use the same `points` for `build_kp` and `build_wind_kp`.

## Accuracy (test/test_windkp.jl: 16 km bay open to the west, 25 → 3 m deep, table 8 directions × 4, 8, 16, 24 m/s)
| check | rms relative Hs error |
|---|---|
| 12 m/s from 270° (speed interpolation only), log-log / linear | 3.4 % / 9.3 % |
| 12 m/s from 250° (speed and direction), log-log / linear | 3.5 % / 9.9 % |
| swell (Hs 1 m, Tp 8 s from 260°) alone vs direct run | 0.0 % |
| swell + wind sea (12 m/s, 250°) vs one coupled direct run | 6.4 % (15.4 % if the wind sea is ignored) |

The log-log curve is much better than the linear one: wind-sea m0 grows like a power of U (in a 20 km fetch, 20 m deep
run: m0 ∝ U^2.5 between 5 and 10 m/s, U^2.7 between 10 and 20 m/s), which a straight line between trained speeds cuts under.

## Assumptions and limits
* **Uniform, steady wind**: one wind vector per time over the whole domain, blowing long enough for the fetch-limited
  stationary state (fine for small basins; for a large basin with a short wind event the duration limit is ignored).
* **Swell and wind sea are independent**. In the model they interact through whitecapping (it depends on the steepness of
  the total sea) and breaking, both non-linear: that is the 6 % above. Build the swell coefficients with the wind off.
* **Offshore wind sea**: the offshore spectrum given to `reconstruct` already contains the wind sea generated outside the
  domain; it is propagated by the swell coefficients. The table only adds growth inside the domain, starting from zero at
  the boundary, so with an onshore wind the wind sea at the boundary is counted once (offshore) and the growth over the
  domain adds to it rather than continuing it: expect a slight under-estimate for long onshore fetches across the domain.
* Training cost: `length(dirs) × length(speeds)` stationary runs. The solver's absolute tolerance is set to 0.1 mm by
  default: the usual 5 mm lets a light-wind sea stop growing early (half the converged Hs at 5 m/s in a test).
* The wind input / whitecapping constants are those of `WaveAction` (see the caveat in `WaveParams`).
